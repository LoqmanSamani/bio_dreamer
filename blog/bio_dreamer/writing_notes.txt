
1. World Models & JEPA (Backbone Architecture)


[1] (project_literature.md: 1.1 Causal-JEPA — Learning World Models through Object-Level Latent Interventions) (refs/causalgepa_1.pdf)
---
Causal-JEPA (C-JEPA) serves as a foundational architectural blueprint for world modeling by extending 
the Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) with a causal inductive bias. By transitioning from 
image-patch masking to object-level masking, the model is forced to infer an object's latent trajectory 
from the evolving states of others, effectively inducing latent interventions with counterfactual-like effects. 
This strategy prevents shortcut solutions like simple temporal interpolation, making interaction reasoning 
functionally necessary for minimizing the prediction objective. The training objective combines a mask 
reconstruction term for history and a forward prediction term for the future, which can be viewed through 
the lens of the Free Energy Principle as minimizing prediction error in a structured latent space. 
BioDreamer adopts this framework to treat biological perturbations—such as mutations or drug treatments as latent 
interventions that the world model can "dream" and plan against entirely in representation space.

(imgs/fig1.png) figure 1: Object-level latent interventions in C-JEPA. Selected object slots are masked across time, except for a minimal 
          identity anchor, forcing the predictor to infer object dynamics from interactions with other objects and 
          auxiliary variables.

The model minimizes the following masked latent prediction objective:
Lmask​=E[τ∈T∑​i=1∑N​1[zˉτi​=zτi​]∣∣z^τi​−zτi​∣∣22​]



[2](project_literature.md: 1.2 LeWorldModel — Stable End-to-End JEPA from Pixels) (refs/leworldmodel_2.pdf)
---
LeWorldModel (LeWM) provides the practical training recipe for BioDreamer by solving the notorious instability 
of Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs). It replaces complex heuristics with a simple, two-term objective: 
a mean-squared error (MSE) for next-latent prediction and the SIGReg regularizer, which prevents representation collapse 
by enforcing an isotropic Gaussian distribution (zt​∼N(0,I)). This streamlined approach allows BioDreamer to learn world 
models end-to-end from raw data on a single GPU. Crucially, LeWM demonstrates that latent-space prediction is more 
efficient than reconstruction-based methods like DreamerV3, enabling planning speeds up to 48x faster while maintaining 
a compact parameter count.

(imgs/fig2.png) figure 2: LeWorldModel Training Pipeline. The encoder maps observations ot​ into low-dimensional latents zt​, 
          while the predictor autoregressively forecasts the next state zt+1​. To prevent the model from collapsing 
          into trivial solutions, the SIGReg term enforces feature diversity by projecting latents onto random 
          directions and applying a normality test to ensure the distribution matches an isotropic Gaussian.


[3] (1.3 DIAMOND — Diffusion for World Modeling) (refs/diamond_3.pdf)
---
DIAMOND (DIffusion As a Model Of eNvironment Dreams) serves as a key precedent for BioDreamer's Latent Diffusion JEPA variant. 
It demonstrates that diffusion-based world models can capture critical visual details and complex, multi-modal transitions 
that are often lost in discrete latent models like DreamerV3. By iteratively reversing a noising process, 
DIAMOND produces crisp, stable rollouts over long horizons, effectively mitigating the compounding errors 
typical of deterministic or simple one-step predictors. While DIAMOND operates directly in observation (pixel) 
space for high-fidelity "neural game engines," BioDreamer adapts this approach into a JEPA latent space. 
This transition mirrors the evolution from pixel-space diffusion to Latent Diffusion (e.g., Stable Diffusion), 
providing the massive efficiency gains necessary for simulating complex biological multi-step strategies in imagination.


[4] (1.4 DreamerV3 — Mastering Diverse Domains Through World Models) (refs/dreamer_4.pdf)
---
DreamerV3 is the flagship RSSM-based world-model RL agent and serves as the primary inspiration for BioDreamer. 
It proved that a single, robust architecture could master over 150 diverse environments—ranging from the visual 
complexity of Minecraft to high-precision robotics—using a single set of hyperparameters. While BioDreamer retains 
DreamerV3’s core principles of imagination-based policy training and symlog-based robustness, it replaces the 
reconstruction-heavy RSSM backbone with a JEPA-based world model. This shift eliminates the need for a decoder during 
planning and integrates an energy-based objective that aligns with Active Inference and intervention-aware prediction. 
In the BioDreamer library, DreamerV3 stands as the essential historical baseline against which these newer latent-space 
innovations are measured.

(imgs/fig3.png) figure 3: Training process of Dreamer.
                          The world model (a) encodes sensory inputs into discrete representations zt​ which are reconstructed
                          to ensure they contain informative features, while a sequence model ht​ learns to predict these 
                          representations given past actions. Simultaneously, the actor and critic (b) learn optimal behaviors 
                          by predicting actions at​ and values vt​ from trajectories of abstract representations "dreamed" or 
                          imagined by the world model.



[5] (1.5 Ha & Schmidhuber — World Models (Original)) (refs/worldmodel_5.pdf)
---
This is the seminal paper that popularized "world models" in the modern deep reinforcement learning sense, 
establishing the core conceptual framework that BioDreamer inherits. It introduces a tripartite architecture 
consisting of a VAE for spatial compression, an MDN-RNN for temporal dynamics, and a compact controller for decision-making. 
By demonstrating that an agent can be trained entirely within its own "hallucinated dream" and then successfully transferred 
to the real environment, it proved the viability of in-silico planning. While BioDreamer swaps the VAE for a JEPA and the RNN 
for more advanced transformers, it maintains this fundamental philosophy: learn to compress the world, dream of its possibilities, 
and only then act.





2. Active Inference & Free Energy Principle (Theoretical Backbone)


[6] (2.1 Active Inference: The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, and Behavior) (could not download the text book!)
---
could not read the book!

**Why it matters:** The definitive textbook. Covers expected free energy (EFE) decomposition into pragmatic value 
(reward) + epistemic value (information gain). BioDreamer adapts this framework: the world model is the generative model, 
mutations/perturbations are policies, and EFE drives action selection across all three pillars.


[7] (2.2 Reinforcement Learning Through Active Inference) (refs/activeinf_7.pdf)
---
This paper provides the technical bridge between theoretical Active Inference and practical deep reinforcement learning. 
It demonstrates how minimizing the Expected Free Energy (EFE) naturally subsumes both reward maximization and information 
gain into a single objective, providing a principled "recipe" for implementing active inference with neural networks. 
For BioDreamer, this framework is transformative: it allows the model to prioritize mutations or perturbations that not 
only move the system toward a desired state (like higher binding affinity) but also target regions of the biological 
landscape where the world model is most uncertain. By treating biological design as an inference problem rather than a 
simple optimization task, BioDreamer achieves a sophisticated balance of exploration and exploitation directly applicable 
to complex, high-dimensional cellular and molecular environments.

The agent selects policies π by minimizing the Expected Free Energy G(π), which can be decomposed into an epistemic (information-seeking) 
term and an extrinsic (goal-seeking) term:

G(\pi) \approx \sum_{\tau} \mathbb{E}_{q(s_\tau, o_\tau|\pi)} [\underbrace{\ln q(s_\tau|\pi) - \ln q(s_\tau|o_\tau, \pi)}_{\text{Epistemic Value}} - \underbrace{\ln p(o_\tau)}_{\text{Extrinsic Value}}]






3. Protein Foundation Models & Structure Prediction


[8](3.1 ESM-2 — Language Models of Protein Evolution) (could not download the paper!)
---
The ESM-2 paper establishes a high-capacity Transformer architecture that serves as a powerful primary sequence encoder 
by implicitly capturing evolutionary and structural constraints from 65 million diverse sequences. For projects like 
ProteinDreamer, the ESM-2 650M variant offers an optimal balance between representation depth and computational efficiency, 
providing dense embeddings where attention maps naturally converge to physical residue-residue contact maps. A key innovation 
for protein design is the use of masked marginals—a zero-shot fitness heuristic that calculates the log-likelihood ratio between 
mutant and wild-type residues at masked positions, offering a robust, label-free reward signal for reinforcement learning. Additionally, 
the paper introduces ESMFold, which leverages these learned internal representations to predict atomic-level 3D structures up to 60x faster 
than AlphaFold2 by eliminating the need for multiple sequence alignments (MSAs), thereby enabling the real-time structural "world modeling" 
necessary for iterative de novo design.


[9] (3.2 AlphaFold2 — Highly Accurate Protein Structure Prediction) (refs/alphafold_9.pdf)
---
AlphaFold represents a paradigm shift in structural biology, solving the long-standing protein-folding problem by predicting 3D 
structures with atomic accuracy from amino acid sequences. For BioDreamer, this technology is a critical infrastructure component: 
these high-fidelity predicted structures serve as "cheap" computational stand-ins for expensive experimental data. They are used 
both as the foundational training data for ProteinDreamer’s world model—allowing it to learn the mapping between sequence mutations 
and structural shifts—and as a rapid validation tool to assess the viability of novel proteins designed in imagination before they 
ever reach a wet lab.


[10](3.3 ESM-3 — Simulating 500 Million Years of Evolution with a Language Model) (refs/esm-3_10.pdf)
---
ESM3 represents the current frontier of multimodal protein foundation models, capable of reasoning simultaneously across sequence, 
structure, and function tokens. It serves as a powerful "evolutionary simulator" that can generate novel, functional proteins 
through complex, one-shot prompting. For BioDreamer, ESM3 defines the state-of-the-art in generative design; 
while ESM3 excels at generating high-quality candidates in a single pass, ProteinDreamer’s world-model-guided 
approach is designed to be complementary—providing the iterative, closed-loop reasoning and "mental simulation" 
needed to refine these candidates through multi-step latent interventions.

4. Generative Protein Design (One-Shot Baselines)


[11] (4.1 ProteinMPNN — Robust Deep Learning–Based Protein Sequence Design) (could not download the paper)
---
Dauparas et al. present ProteinMPNN, a deep learning–based inverse-folding model that represents the dominant paradigm in modern protein design: given a fixed backbone structure,
the model predicts a compatible amino acid sequence in a single forward pass. By learning structural and geometric constraints from large datasets of known proteins, 
ProteinMPNN efficiently generates sequences likely to fold into the target backbone, achieving strong performance without iterative refinement or explicit search. 
This one-shot backbone-to-sequence mapping makes it a powerful and fast baseline, but also limits it to static structure-conditioned design. As such, it can naturally 
serve as a sub-module within iterative or generative frameworks—e.g., for re-scoring or redesigning sequences after each step—highlighting how approaches like ProteinDreamer 
extend beyond this paradigm by incorporating sequential decision-making and exploration over protein space.


[12] (4.2 RFdiffusion — De Novo Protein Design with Diffusion Models) (rfdiff_12.pdf)
---
Watson et al. introduce RFdiffusion, a generative model that produces novel protein backbones by iteratively denoising 3D 
coordinate representations starting from random noise . The method demonstrates strong capability across a wide range of 
design tasks—including de novo monomers, protein binders, symmetric assemblies, and functional motif scaffolds—showing 
that diffusion-based generative models can successfully learn the distribution of protein structures and produce physically 
realistic backbones at scale. However, the approach operates as a single generative process per design, without explicit 
iterative optimization, reinforcement learning, or navigation of a fitness landscape. As such, RFdiffusion provides compelling 
evidence that generative modeling is effective for protein design, while leaving open the opportunity for frameworks like 
ProteinDreamer to build on this foundation by introducing iterative planning, sequential decision-making, and adaptive 
exploration over protein space.


[13] (4.3 EvoDiff — Protein Generation via Sequence Diffusion) (refs/evodiff_13.pdf)
---
EvoDiff introduces a powerful paradigm for protein engineering by demonstrating that high-quality, 
functional proteins can be generated using diffusion models directly in sequence space. While most 
generative models rely on 3D structural coordinates, EvoDiff proves that "sequence is all you need" 
to capture the underlying constraints of the protein fitness landscape. By treating protein generation 
as a discrete diffusion process over amino acids, it provides a highly efficient alternative for designing 
proteins where experimental structures are unavailable. In the BioDreamer framework, this validates the 
feasibility of navigating the protein world model using discrete latent transitions, ensuring that the 
system remains robust even when structural data is sparse or computationally expensive to resolve.





5. RL for Biological Sequence Design


[14] (5.1 Accelerating Protein Engineering with Fitness Landscape Modelling and RL (μFormer + μSearch)) (refs/uformer_14.pdf)
---
μProtein represents the current state-of-the-art in combining static fitness predictors with reinforcement learning for protein engineering. 
The framework uses a specialized deep learning model, μFormer, to map sequences to functional scores, which then serves as a stationary 
"oracle" reward for μSearch, a PPO-based RL algorithm. While effective at identifying high-gain multi-point mutants by navigating 
the rugged fitness landscape, μProtein operates as a traditional "generate-and-test" search in observation space rather than dreaming 
in a latent world model. It lacks the causal latent-space interventions and predictive "imagination" of BioDreamer, positioning 
it as a powerful point-prediction baseline that BioDreamer extends by adding internal world-simulation and Active Inference-driven exploration.





[15] (5.3 Biological Sequence Design with GFlowNets) (refs/gflownets_15.pdf)
---
Jain et al. (2022) introduce GFlowNets (Generative Flow Networks) as a powerful framework for biological sequence design, 
specifically designed to address the limitations of standard Reinforcement Learning (RL) in high-dimensional, multi-modal fitness landscapes. 
Unlike traditional RL agents that seek to maximize a point estimate of reward—often leading to "mode collapse" on a few sequences—GFlowNets 
are trained to sample diverse candidates with a probability proportional to their reward (or fitness). In benchmarks against existing methods 
like DynaPPO, GFlowNets consistently generated batches of sequences with higher structural diversity and novelty while maintaining high predicted 
activity across tasks such as antimicrobial peptide (AMP) and DNA-binding protein design. For the ProteinDreamer proposal, GFlowNets represent 
a state-of-the-art alternative policy class; while the ProteinDreamer world model provides the predictive "environment" and thermodynamic constraints, 
GFlowNets can serve as the policy engine to explore this environment, ensuring that the design process does not get trapped in narrow local optima and 
instead identifies a broad, diverse repertoire of viable protein candidates.






6. MolDreamer Foundations (Molecular Dynamics ML)

[16] (6.1 MACE — Higher Order Equivariant Message Passing for Force Fields) (refs/mace_16.pdf)
---
MACE (Multi-ACE) is a state-of-the-art equivariant graph neural network (GNN) that provides the high-precision interatomic potentials 
required for MolDreamer’s force-field calculations. It addresses a major limitation of traditional message-passing neural networks (MPNNs)—which 
typically rely on simple two-body messages—by introducing higher-order body messages (e.g., four-body). This architectural innovation allows MACE 
to achieve superior accuracy and generalization with only two message-passing iterations, significantly reducing computational cost and increasing 
parallelizability compared to models requiring five or six layers. By leveraging an efficient tensor-product structure to circumvent exponential scaling, 
MACE provides a systematic and fast framework for learning equivariant mappings in complex molecular environments.
The core innovation is the efficient construction of higher-order features Bi​ from two-body features Ai​ using a product of tensor expansions:

[17] (6.2 Timewarp — Transferable Acceleration of Molecular Dynamics) (refs/timewrap_17.pdf)
---
Molecular  dynamics(MD)  simulation is  a  widely used  technique to  simulatemolecular systems, 
most commonly at the all-atom resolution where equations ofmotion are integrated with timesteps on 
the order of femtoseconds (1fs= 10−15s).MD is often used to compute equilibrium properties, 
which requires samplingfrom an equilibrium distribution such as the Boltzmann distribution.  
However, many important processes, such as binding and folding, occur over timescalesof milliseconds or beyond, 
and cannot be efficiently sampled with conventionalMD. Furthermore, new MD simulations need to be performed for 
each molecularsystem studied.
We presentTimewarp, an enhanced sampling method which uses anormalising flow as a proposal distribution in a Markov chain Monte Carlo 
methodtargeting the Boltzmann distribution. The flow is trained offline on MD trajectoriesand learns to make large steps in time,  
simulating the molecular dynamics of105−106fs. Crucially, Timewarp istransferablebetween molecular systems: oncetrained, 
we show that it generalises to unseen small peptides (2-4 amino acids)at all-atom resolution, exploring their metastable 
states and providing wall-clockacceleration of sampling compared to standard MD. Our method constitutes animportant step towards general, 
transferable algorithms for accelerating MD.

Timewarp provides the technical proof-of-concept for MolDreamer’s variable-length latent dynamics. Traditional Molecular Dynamics (MD) is bottlenecked 
by tiny femtosecond timesteps, but Timewarp uses normalizing flows to learn "time-coarsened" jumps, allowing the model to transition directly 
between distant conformational states. By training on MD trajectories, it learns a proposal distribution for a Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) 
sampler that targets the Boltzmann distribution, ensuring physical correctness while accelerating exploration by orders of magnitude. 
This demonstrates that we can move away from integrating Newton's equations step-by-step and instead "dream" significant molecular structural changes, 
making the simulation of long-horizon processes like protein folding or ligand binding computationally tractable.

(imgs/fig4.png) figure 4: Acceleration of Molecular Dynamics via Timewarp.
                          While conventional MD (top) must compute thousands of sequential small steps (δt) to observe a change in molecular conformation, 
                          Timewarp (bottom) learns a stochastic propagator that generates large, discrete "jumps" (Δt) in state space. This learned model 
                          acts as a transferable proposal distribution that significantly speeds up the sampling of equilibrium properties.

To ensure the jumps remain physically valid, the model utilizes a Metropolis-Hastings acceptance step based on the potential energy U(x):

A(x′∣x)=min(1,p(x)q(x′∣x)p(x′)q(x∣x′)​)

where p(x)∝exp(−U(x)/kB​T) is the Boltzmann distribution and q is the learned transition distribution.






7. CellDreamer Foundations (Cellular Perturbation Models)


[18] (7.1 GEARS — Predicting Transcriptional Outcomes of Novel Multigene Perturbations) (refs/gears_18.pdf)
---
For CellDreamer, GEARS (Graph-Enhanced Gene Activation and Repression Simulator) represents the state-of-the-art in predicting transcriptional responses to multigene perturbations. 
It is the first model to successfully predict the outcomes of perturbing gene combinations where one or both genes have never been experimentally perturbed before. 
It achieves this by integrating single-cell RNA-sequencing data with a biological knowledge graph, allowing the model to generalize across the gene interaction network. 
However, GEARS is fundamentally a steady-state predictor; it maps a perturbation to a final expression profile without modeling the underlying temporal 
dynamics or allowing for the multi-step planning required for cellular reprogramming. CellDreamer aims to surpass this by treating the gene regulatory network as a 
dynamic environment, using the predictive power of a world model to "dream" and plan sequences of interventions over time.


(imgs/fig5.png) figure 4: GEARS Architecture for Perturbation Prediction.
The model takes a set of gene perturbations as input and uses a multidimensional gene-gene interaction graph to propagate these signals. By combining a perturbational autoencoder with a knowledge graph, GEARS predicts how the expression of thousands of non-perturbed genes will shift, effectively simulating the transcriptional "fingerprint" of novel genetic interventions.

The model represents the expression of gene i after perturbation of genes a and b as:
yi​=f(zi​+gi,a​+gi,b​)

where zi​ is the basal gene embedding and gi,a​ represents the specific effect of perturbing gene a on gene i, aggregated through the graph.


[19] (7.2 Replogle et al. — Genome-Wide Perturb-seq) (refs/seqdata_19.pdf)
---
This paper presents the first genome-scale Perturb-seq screens, providing the foundational training dataset for CellDreamer. 
By combining CRISPR interference (CRISPRi) with single-cell RNA sequencing, the study maps the transcriptional effects of genetic perturbations 
across >2.5 million human cells. This "information-rich genotype-phenotype map" covers nearly all expressed genes, enabling the identification 
of gene functions and the dissection of complex cellular phenomena like aneuploidy and mitochondrial stress. While previous screens were limited 
to a few hundred targets, this dataset’s massive scale allows CellDreamer to learn the global "rules" of gene regulatory dynamics and plan 
multi-step interventions in a high-dimensional latent space.


The utility of the dataset for unsupervised classification of gene function is highlighted by the fact that while the number of perturbations N 
scales linearly with cost, the potential for discovery scales quadratically:

Utility∝N2 (pairwise comparisons) [cite: 591]






8. Fitness Landscapes & Key Data Sources


[20] (8.1 ProteinGym — Large-Scale Benchmarks for Protein Fitness Prediction and Design) (refs/proteingym_20.pdf)
---
ProteinGym serves as the primary evaluation benchmark for ProteinDreamer, providing a large-scale, standardized platform to assess protein fitness prediction and design.
1. Benchmark Scale and Diversity
The benchmark encompasses over 2.7 million mutated sequences across more than 200 protein families, providing the scale necessary for robust model evaluation. It includes:
    217 substitution DMS assays: High-throughput assays evaluating the functional impact of single or multiple amino acid replacements.
    66 indel assays (DMS): Assessing insertions and deletions, which are technically challenging due to sequence length alterations.
    Assay Diversity: The collection spans diverse taxa (humans, other eukaryotes, prokaryotes, and viruses) and a wide array of functional 
    properties, including ligand binding, aggregation, thermostability, viral replication, and drug resistance.

2. Evaluation Metrics
ProteinGym utilizes a structured analytics framework with metrics tailored to specific tasks:
    Fitness Prediction: Spearman’s rank correlation is the primary metric, as it captures the often non-linear relationship between sequence and fitness. 
    In bimodal cases, AUC and Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) are used to evaluate binarized labels.
    Protein Design: Metrics focus on identifying the most functional variants:
        NDCG (Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain): Up-weights models that correctly rank high-value sequences at the top.
        Top-K Recall: Measures the ability to identify the top 10% of high-fitness sequences.

3. Training Regimes and Splits
The benchmark integrates two complementary settings to evaluate model performance:
    Zero-Shot Setting: Models predict mutation effects without access to ground-truth labels for the protein of interest during training, testing robust generalization.
    Supervised Setting: Models leverage labeled subsets for training. To prevent overestimating performance due to data leakage, ProteinGym employs three cross-validation schemes:
        Random: Mutations are randomly assigned to folds.
        Contiguous: Splits sequences into five contiguous segments to test extrapolation to unseen regions.
        Modulo: Assigns positions to folds based on a modulo operator.



[21] (8.2 Mega-Scale Stability Data — Tsuboyama et al.) (refs/stabdata_21.pdf)
---
The study by Tsuboyama et al. (2023) introduces cDNA display proteolysis, a massively parallel assay that generated a dataset of 776,298 
high-quality absolute folding stability (ΔG) measurements. This dataset, significantly richer than previous thermodynamic databases, 
covers all single amino acid variants (including deletions and insertions) for 331 natural and 148 de novo designed protein domains, 
along with 210,118 selected double mutants across 190 domains. The domains range from 40 to 72 amino acids in length and represent a 
diverse structural space, including nearly all suitable small monomeric PDB domains and various de novo topologies like ααα, αββα, 
and trRosetta hallucinations. The methodology proceeds in four primary steps: 
    (1) Library Synthesis: Synthetic DNA oligonucleotide pools are used to encode up to 900,000 unique protein sequences; 
    (2) cDNA Display: These libraries are transcribed and translated in a cell-free system where each protein is covalently
        linked to its own cDNA at the C-terminus; 
    (3) Protease Challenge: The protein-cDNA complexes are incubated with varying concentrations of two orthogonal proteases, 
        trypsin and chymotrypsin, which preferentially cleave unfolded proteins ; and 
    (4) Quantification: Intact (resistant) proteins are pulled down via an N-terminal tag, and their 
        relative abundance is measured using next-generation sequencing (NGS). 
To ensure low noise and high accuracy, the researchers employed a Bayesian kinetic model to infer ΔG from the protease concentration at which 
half-maximal cleavage occurs (K50​), specifically accounting for sequence-specific protease susceptibility in the unfolded state. 
This rigorous approach resulted in high reproducibility (R=0.94 between independent protease experiments) and strong agreement 
with traditional gold-standard purified protein measurements.






9. Reviews (Big-Picture Framing)


[22] (9.2 Adaptive Machine Learning for Protein Engineering) (refs/rew_22.pdf)
---
This paper provides a high-level synthesis of the adaptive machine learning loop that has become the standard for protein engineering: 
a cycle of training a sequence-to-function surrogate model, using it to navigate combinatorial complexity to propose new designs, 
and retraining the model with experimental results. While this iterative design process is already widely practiced by the community, 
BioDreamer provides a rigorous formalization of this loop. By replacing static surrogate models with dynamic world models and replacing 
simple optimization with Active Inference, BioDreamer moves beyond "one-shot" predictions. This framework enables the agent to explicitly 
account for model uncertainty and plan multi-step experimental strategies that maximize both information gain and functional performance.

                          This model then guides the selection of novel candidates from a vast combinatorial space for experimental measurement, 
                          the results of which are fed back into the system to refine the model’s accuracy and predictive power in subsequent rounds.
